🌡️ No fleet vs today’s global warming

No fleet and no greenhouse gases: the red value above is the projected equilibrium temperature with reflectors disabled and greenhouse trapping removed. It still includes sunlight absorption and the model’s temperature-dependent albedo.

Today’s observed climate: the World Meteorological Organization reports that the global mean temperature in 2025 was about 1.43 °C above the 1850–1900 average. This is a real-world global temperature anomaly, not an absolute temperature, and it is not directly comparable to the simulation’s no-fleet equilibrium temperature. Teaching model: global-mean energy balance. Absorbed light = S₀/d² × (1 + reflector area × 0.9 ÷ Earth's disc) × (1 − albedo) ÷ 4. Greenhouse: one grey infrared layer, surface T⁴ = Tₑ⁴(1 + ¾τ), with τ from water vapour (Clausius–Clapeyron, freezes out when cold), CO₂ (logarithmic) and other gases, plus ice–albedo feedback and a 7×10⁸ J/m²/K ocean layer. The reflector fleet is normally sized from this energy balance (feedforward + integral trim) to hold 15 °C. Auto 1:4 mode adjusts the model’s effective CO₂ mixing ratio to represent 20% of the current heat deficit as greenhouse trapping and provides the remaining 80% with reflector-absorbed sunlight; the reflector area and effective CO₂ are updated as distance and baseline CO₂ change. Extreme effective concentrations are mathematical teaching-model equivalents, not realistic atmospheric forecasts. Distance, and the simulated years per second, come from the orbit simulation. Not a forecast: no seasons, latitude, deep ocean or beam logistics.
